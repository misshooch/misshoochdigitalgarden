Dearest, 


>Today, I did my weekly shop, which is about three kilometres from where I live. It was raining on the walk back, and my bag broke, so I called an Uber after I picked up all my mushrooms off the floor. 

>I got chatting to the driver, who was impressed that I usually walk six kilometres round-trip to do my shopping. I told him that it's good to move.

>Anyway, we started talking. He was raised Muslim, is originally from Pakistan, and is an astrophysicist who has recently started experimenting with drugs. We ended up continuing our conversation for about fifteen minutes after he'd dropped me home, just sitting in the car talking. He was kind and he saw me as kind too. There was a wonderful reciprocity to our conversation.

>He's relatively inexperienced with drugs, whereas I am relatively experienced;) I told him about my history of taking hero doses of mushrooms, and he called me 'a boss' then 'the boss' (which I liked) and listened to what I had to say. He seemed genuinely interested in trying to make sense of it all.

>He considers himself agnostic and was asking me about my beliefs, my opinions, and the conclusions I've come to about the world. It's interesting to be that I often have DEEPLY thought-provoking and contemplative conversations with my uber drivers. One uber drive I had once... I think was an oracle. 

>When I was a child I would stay up all night trying to imagine infinity. It would cause me to have meltdowns. Now I think I feel a peace that I understand this experience on earth, at least for me. A LOT of my drug trips, be it in k-holes or otherwise, often came back to theological and philosophical motifs - which is why I developed a novel interest. I remember some really life changing ones as to the nature of free will and life after death. I have no fear of death at all and I am grateful for the drugs for that! 


> [!NOTE] Sidenote
> I wish so deeply that I had studied philosophy. 


>I suppose I feel quite satisfied with my level of understanding and knowledge of the world around me, but I found some of his questions quite confronting. There were things I considered saying but ultimately didn't, because I worried they might come across as tactless or tone-deaf.

>It reminded me of something that happened when I first moved here.

>When I couldn't find anywhere to live, I ended up going to Bali whilst I waited for a room, which I actually really didn't like. I was quite critical of it. To me, it felt like a place that had been colonised by capitalism, amongst other things.

>Anyway, while I was there, I met a Russian-Israeli girl (:////////////) on the beach who told me that my problem was that I felt too much shame and could see everybody's point of view. She's becoming IG and Pintrest famous now which makes me so sad...

>There was a man on the beach who appeared to be on drugs and was staring at her. She said she was going to call the police and have him sent to jail, and I tried to discourage her. I understood why she felt uncomfortable - he was leering and oogling. BUT, I also perceived us as the physical embodiments of his generational trauma. We and the people we respresent are responsible for completely destabilising the local population, erasing their land, culture and traditions. In that context, I thought her response was rather unkind but, it didn't justify the sexual undertones to his behaviour. 

>She had this belief system, which I think was rooted in New Age spirituality, that everything was ultimately fine. 

>Essentially, her position was that people deserve whatever happens to them. If somebody is having a bad life, it's because they deserve to have a bad life.

>And I think that's where things become difficult for me.

>My own understanding of the world and the universe doesn't echo what she was saying, but I can see how certain conclusions I might draw could be interpreted in a similar way.

>For example, the Uber driver was talking about what's happening to people in Gaza, which is, of course, horrendous. But I also found myself thinking about the fact that he's from Pakistan, and how Western narratives about life in Pakistan might lead somebody to believe that living there is uniformly terrible.

>I wanted to bring this up, but then I worried that doing so might diminish the experiences and suffering of people in Gaza.

>I think it's incredibly difficult to navigate the world in a way that is politically correct while also maintaining a coherent personal belief system.

>And I suppose this is, fundamentally, part of why the Abrahamic religions seem to be crumbling in a way they haven't before. Historically, I think war was perhaps more widely accepted or justified by general consensus.

>Or perhaps not.

>Perhaps that's simply the historiography of it all. Maybe I'm being anachronistic.

>That's why these things are so nebulous. I suppose we'll never really know. This is why I'm also distrustful of museums but I think that might be a bit schzio of me. 

>Anyway, it exercised my brain a little bit, which was nice.

>It was lovely to feel that I could be of service to the Uber driver. I really hope he gets his Valium. It sounds like he's going to have a lovely time.

>We were talking about DMT as well, and I told him about one particularly ridiculous occasion when I'd taken seven grams of mushrooms, two tabs of acid, smoked DMT, and also taken MDMA, cocaine, ketamine, alcohol, speed and maybe 2CB.

>Pretty bad. Pretty bad combination.

>I'm pleased that I'm no longer continuing to do the damage to my brain that I've obviously already done.

>Anyway, I had a big cry yesterday, but I feel better today. A lot better, actually. (also my mum gave me money for food which I was economical with and then bought some clothes which have made me feel :D (hard to justify but last longer than food and i needed. trousers as I didn't really have any))

>Then I went to AA.

>My 82-year-old bestie told me that there's been a dark cloud hanging over him and that he's ready to go.

>To which I said, "Fuck off."

>He was hurt that I hadn't called him this week, and his ex-wife, whom he's still very close to, is dying. I think I need to make more of an effort to be available to him.

>But I've been struggling.

>And I feel as though I'm struggling more often than I'm not at the moment, so I don't really know where the line is. How much can I reasonably give when I'm finding things difficult myself?

>Idk. I'll have to make more of an effort to reach out.

>As I left he pulled me to the side and poked me and told me I was special. When people say these kinds of things to me, I tend to believe them - which is why I have an unironically inflated ego. 

>Anyway, I texted him afterwards to thank him for everything, and he replied, "I love you too."

>Which was very kind. Very kind.

>He's so cool. Dripped out. One iced out guy. Tuff outfits always. Will insert pic of today. 

>I think that's about it for today.

>Although, my mum is probably right. I think I do think too much.

>I've always regarded that as some kind of indication of my intelligence, but perhaps it isn't. Perhaps I just have a very busy brain, and perhaps all that mental energy would be better directed elsewhere.

>Anyway, I'm having some very intense feelings come over me physically at the moment, and I'm very excited by them.

>I am EXTREMELY enjoying my friend Raisa. I haven't had a friendship with a person like her before. It is a lot less declarative than I am accustomed to. But, she is reliable and does the things I am so used to doing. She is so much fun. I laughed so much with her before I nearly vomitted. This friendship has been slow and steady and largely initiated by her which I am pleased about. I like to be pursued now I think. She is really opening my eyes to many possibilities and also gender roles. I feel conflicted. I think I like some of her views a lot. I worry I may be becoming... influenced?

>Can't a girl just catch everything she deserves and more?
